# CrownCode orbital field studies

> **Archived (2026-09-25).** The homepage no longer uses these photographs as
> page art: it renders the CrownCode Atlas in WebGL (procedural worlds). The
> files now live in `assets-src/showroom-archive/` (not deployed). Atlas
> assets are generated, not drawn: `scripts/generate-atlas-textures.py`
> (sky + crown sprite) and `scripts/capture-atlas-assets.py` (poster and
> world thumbnails rendered from the scene itself).


Built-in image generation, 2026-09-25. Both exports are 1536 × 1024 WebP, quality 80. The authentic CrownCode glyph and project worlds belong in the page layer, not in these images.

## `orbital-field-v3.webp` (preferred)

> Use case: stylized-concept. Asset type: wide landscape 3:2 background plate for a premium interactive software-project planetarium. A single continuous orbital environment as if observed from a quiet spacecraft: the curvature of an enormous dark planetary orbital plane, tilted 28 degrees to the horizon and receding in perspective, suggested only by several hair-thin fragments of aged brass trajectory lines and a broad nearly invisible band of particulate shadow. A huge still charcoal void through the center for independently animated project planets and a separately overlaid real crown logo. Subtle desaturated slate-blue black in the far depth; one restrained warm edge light from upper left; physically plausible, cinematic matte film exposure with slight silver-grain imperfections. Minimal and solemn, like an astronomical photograph paired with precise scientific engraving, not fantasy art. No actual planets, spheres, moons, stars, nebula, galaxies, meteor dust sparkles, glowing rings, desk, floor, printed paper, text, numerals, diagrams, logos, fake glyphs, computers, products or UI. No obvious repeating texture. Do not make luminous gold swirls or a stock sci-fi wallpaper. All perspective and light must feel part of one environment.

The result has a single diagonal light horizon and dark central space. Its restrained surface texture survived WebP export. It is deliberately sparse so SVG trajectories and real project images can carry the scene.

## `orbital-archive-v3.webp` (alternate)

> Use case: stylized-concept. Asset type: wide 3:2 full-bleed CrownCode portfolio map background, designed to sit behind animated planets and an authentic overlaid crown glyph. Create a single expansive astronomical image, not a collage: a beautifully restrained 1970s observatory astrometric plate / intaglio print made with lampblack ink on very dark graphite paper, photographed straight enough for a web backdrop but with the orbital plane strongly tilted diagonally into depth. Fine incomplete bronze and pale grey orbital trajectories sweep asymmetrically from lower left toward upper right, with irregular handmade line weight and occasional subtle registration marks only at the extreme edges. Imagine celestial mechanics sketched by a meticulous engineer: physically coherent perspective, enormous scale, 70% calm dark negative space in the center for independently animated project worlds. Palette charcoal ink, restrained aged brass, hint of desaturated slate blue, bone-white highlights only at a few hairline crossings. Keep the darkest area genuinely deep so cream interface copy reads. Warm grazing light from upper left, but no bright wash. Tactile matte print grain, exquisite editorial art direction, avoid digital gradients and CGI sheen. Absolutely no planets, spheres, stars, galaxies, nebula, explosions, haze, mountain/stone/ground texture, desks, corners, borders, machines, physical products, text, letters, digits, logos, icons, watermarks, UI or generic sci-fi effects. Deliberate sophisticated negative space; all line geometry should be plausible as one continuous tilted orbital system.

The alternate has more overt cartographic marks. Use it only if the page's own orbital SVG is reduced enough to avoid duplicate paths.
